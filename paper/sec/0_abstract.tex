\begin{abstract}
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies that re-plan at every control step pay the full cost of the model at every step.
This report studies where that cost goes, and which ways of reducing it preserve closed-loop success, for the released LIBERO checkpoint of SmolVLA on a Tesla T4.
The released configuration needs 789\,ms per re-plan, 24 times the simulator step.
Two overheads dominate: bfloat16 arithmetic, which Turing GPUs emulate, and the 10-step flow-matching loop of the action expert, whose small kernels leave the GPU waiting on kernel launches.
Methods are compared on identical episodes with common random numbers and paired tests.
Re-planning less often fails: executing 5 actions per chunk, re-planning at predicted gripper switches, or reusing a 5-step-old image all lower success by 12.0--12.5 points (Holm-adjusted $p\le0.012$).
Re-planning more cheaply succeeds: float16 weights, 3 Euler steps and a CUDA-graph replay of the flow loop cut a re-plan to 112\,ms ($7.0\times$); the graph is bit-exact, and success is unchanged on 200 paired episodes (77.0\% vs 77.0\%).

\end{abstract}
