\begin{abstract}
A vision-language-action (VLA) policy that re-plans at every control step pays for the whole model at every step.
For the released LIBERO checkpoint of SmolVLA on a Tesla T4, this report measures where the cost goes and which reductions keep closed-loop success on identical, paired episodes.
A re-plan takes 789\,ms, dominated by emulated bfloat16 and a launch-bound 10-step flow loop.
Re-planning less often (open-loop chunks, gripper-event triggers, a cached image) lowers success by about 12 points.
Re-planning more cheaply keeps it: float16 with 3 Euler steps is non-inferior on 500 paired episodes (78.4\% vs 75.4\%, 95\% CI of the difference $[-0.6, +6.6]$ points), and with a CUDA-graph replay that returns the same actions a re-plan takes 112\,ms ($7.1\times$); one Euler step (93\,ms, $8.5\times$) is non-inferior on 300 fresh episodes.
One flow noise per episode removes gripper chatter but lowers success from 77.0\% to 23.5\%.
\end{abstract}
