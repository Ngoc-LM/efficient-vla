\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Computational bottlenecks.}
\cref{tab:profile} breaks down one re-plan (CUDA events, warm-up, median of 60 calls).
The simulator is not the bottleneck (32\,ms per step against 814\,ms for the policy).
The vision encoder takes 230\,ms in the released bfloat16 weights but 39\,ms in float16: Turing GPUs have no bfloat16 tensor cores and emulate it.
The action expert accounts for 18\% of the FLOPs of a re-plan but 63\% of its latency ($10\times51$\,ms), and its step time is the same in both precisions: a pass over 50 action tokens is only 11.5\,GFLOP but launches several hundred kernels, so at batch one the GPU waits on launches rather than arithmetic.

\paragraph{Successful and failed trajectories.}
Successes finish in a median of 100 steps; all 46 failures run to the 280-step limit after at least one grasp.
From the executed gripper commands (object poses are not logged), 28 failures release the bowl off target after one or two grasps, 9 still hold it at the limit, and 9 grasp three or more times; task~3 (bowl on the cookie box) fails in 10 of 20 episodes.
Fresh flow noise at every re-plan makes consecutive chunks differ about as much as independent samples (first normalised action: 1.31 from the shifted previous chunk, 1.37 between two noises; 581 LIBERO-Object steps).
The gripper command chatters accordingly: failed episodes contain 12.6 gripper segments of at most 3 steps per 100 steps, successful ones 2.7.

\paragraph{Strengths.}
\textbf{S1 Small footprint:} 1.16\,GB of weights and 21\,MB of activations per re-plan (float16) fit a 16\,GB T4 with room for several processes.
\textbf{S2 Prefix caching:} the expert reuses the cached prefix, so a flow step costs 52\,ms against 304\,ms for the prefix.
\textbf{S3 Few-step flow:} with $S{=}3$ the first action deviates from $S{=}10$ by 0.32, a quarter of the sampling spread (1.37), and SR holds down to $S{=}1$ (\cref{sec:improve}).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\textbf{L1 Precision mismatch:} bfloat16 weights run emulated on Turing.
\textbf{L2 Launch-bound expert:} the flow loop dominates latency at batch one while using little arithmetic.
\textbf{L3 Need for fresh images:} executing 5 actions per chunk, or re-planning with fresh proprioception but a 5-step-old image, loses 12.0--12.5 points (\cref{sec:improve}), so an episode needs a full re-plan at every step.
\textbf{L4 A fresh sample at every step:} independent noise at every re-plan produces gripper chatter, concentrated in failures, although removing it does not raise SR (\cref{sec:improve}).
