\section{Improvement: Re-plan Cheaper, not Less}
\label{sec:improve}

\paragraph{Bottleneck and hypotheses.}
A successful episode (100 steps) spends about 79\,s in the policy and 3\,s in the simulator.
\textbf{H1 (re-plan less):} computation can be shared across steps without lowering SR, by executing $k$ actions per chunk (open loop), by also re-planning when the next queued action switches the gripper, or by re-planning with fresh proprioception on image and language keys and values cached for 5 steps (stale image).
\textbf{H2 (re-plan more cheaply):} each re-plan can be made cheaper while computing the same function up to precision and discretisation: float16 removes the emulated arithmetic (same weights, projections kept in float32), $S{=}3$ removes discretisation slack (S3 in \cref{sec:analysis}), and a CUDA graph removes the launches of the expert loop, which should gain more once precision is fixed.
For the graph, the prefix runs eagerly and the $S$-step Euler loop is captured once per $S$ with static buffers for noise, prefix cache and time steps, then replayed; on isolated calls its actions equal the eager ones exactly in all 8 settings.
A probe tests whether the chatter of L4 matters by reusing one flow noise for every re-plan of an episode (fixed noise).

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Differences in SR are paired (exact McNemar test, Newcombe 95\% CI~\cite{newcombe1998improved}, Holm adjustment~\cite{holm1979simple} over comparisons with the released policy); a variant is non-inferior if the lower bound exceeds $-5$ points, a margin fixed in advance.
Latency is measured on isolated calls, because the closed-loop runs share each GPU between two processes, and checked in the loop with one process per GPU.

\paragraph{Re-planning less lowers success (H1 rejected).}
Calling the vision encoder about five times less often costs 12.0--12.5 points of SR (\cref{tab:sr}); with $k{=}10$ the drop of 9.5 points is not significant after adjustment.
The event trigger removes most chatter (1.2 short gripper segments per 100 steps against 5.0) without recovering success; fresh proprioception does not replace a current image.
Unlike a SmolVLA trained on LIBERO with longer horizons~\cite{sendai2025leave}, this checkpoint ($n_\text{exec}{=}1$) needs a re-plan at every step.

\paragraph{Re-planning more cheaply preserves success (H2).}
Along released $\to$ float16 $\to$ $S{=}3$ $\to$ graph, a re-plan takes 789, 616, 245 and 112\,ms, factors of 1.28, 2.51 and 2.19 ($7.05\times$ in total; \cref{tab:latency}).
By the fits, an Euler step costs 51--53\,ms eagerly in either precision (launch-bound) but 28.3 and 9.4\,ms with the graph, which exposes the emulated arithmetic: the graph gains $1.38\times$ in bfloat16 and $3.46\times$ in float16 at $S{=}10$.
On 200 pairs the lower bound for float16 $S{=}3$ ($-5.8$) misses the margin; on the planned 500 pairs (initial states 0--49) it succeeds in 78.4\% against 75.4\% ($+3.0$ points, 95\% CI $[-0.6, +6.6]$; task-cluster bootstrap $[-1.2, +8.4]$): non-inferior, with a non-significant gain ($p{=}0.13$).
Fewer steps do not lower SR measurably either ($S{=}2$: 77.5\%, $S{=}1$: 79.5\%), and on 300 fresh pairs (initial states 20--49, a confirmation fixed in advance) $S{=}1$ succeeds in \pending{X\%} against \pending{Y\%} (\pending{CI}) at 93\,ms per re-plan ($8.5\times$).
In the loop, a float16 $S{=}3$ re-plan takes 275\,ms eagerly and 114\,ms with the graph, close to the isolated values.
Replay keeps the first action of every episode; whole trajectories match the eager ones in 5 of 10 episodes, as two eager runs do in 10 of 20: execution is not bit-reproducible, and the loop amplifies small differences.

\paragraph{Consistent noise is not the remedy.}
Fixed noise removes the chatter (0.5 against 3.8 short gripper segments per 100 steps) but lowers SR from 77.0\% to 23.5\% (111 against 4 discordant pairs, $p<10^{-27}$), while $S{=}1$ removes it too (0.7) without lowering SR: chatter accompanies failures without causing them.
A reading consistent with both, not tested here: fresh noise gives errors that average out over steps, fixed noise repeats one error at every step, and $S{=}1$ gives almost none, since it returns $\boldsymbol\epsilon-v_\theta(\boldsymbol\epsilon,1\mid\mathbf o)$ and the minimiser of \cref{eq:fm} at $\tau{=}1$ is $v=\boldsymbol\epsilon-\mathbb E[\mathbf A\mid\mathbf o]$.

\paragraph{Trade-offs and next bottleneck.}
The float16 gain is specific to GPUs without bfloat16 tensor cores; elsewhere only the step and graph gains would apply (not measured).
Float16 changes actions by 0.010 on average (0.049 at most, magnitude 0.17) without overflow; the graph needs static shapes and one capture per $S$.
At 112\,ms per re-plan plus 32\,ms of simulation the loop runs at about 7\,Hz, below the 20\,Hz control rate.
The vision-language prefix (84\,ms) takes 75\% of a re-plan at $S{=}3$ and 90\% at $S{=}1$; further gains require a cheaper prefix, which changes the model and needs training.
