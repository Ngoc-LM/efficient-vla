\section{Improvement: Cheaper, not Less, Re-planning}
\label{sec:improve}

\paragraph{Bottleneck and hypotheses.}
A successful episode (100 steps) spends about 79\,s in the policy and 3\,s in the simulator.
\textbf{H1 (re-plan less):} computation can be shared across steps without lowering SR, by executing $k$ actions per chunk (open loop), by also re-planning when the next queued action switches the gripper, or by re-planning with fresh proprioception on cached image and language keys and values refreshed every 5 steps (stale image).
\textbf{H2 (re-plan more cheaply):} each re-plan can be made cheaper while computing the same function up to precision and discretisation: float16 removes the emulated arithmetic (same weights, projections kept in float32); $S{=}3$ removes discretisation slack (S3 in \cref{sec:analysis}); and a CUDA graph removes the launches of the expert loop.
For the graph, the prefix runs eagerly and the $S$-step Euler loop is captured once per $S$ (0.25--2.2\,s) with static buffers for noise, prefix cache and time steps, then replayed; its actions equal the eager ones exactly in all 8 settings.
H2 predicts SR within the margin and a larger graph speed-up once precision is fixed, since launch overhead hides arithmetic.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Differences in SR are paired (exact McNemar test, Newcombe 95\% CI~\cite{newcombe1998improved}, Holm adjustment~\cite{holm1979simple} over comparisons with the released policy); a variant is non-inferior if the lower bound exceeds $-5$ points, a margin fixed in advance.
Latency is measured on isolated calls, because the closed-loop runs share each GPU between two processes.

\paragraph{Re-planning less lowers success (H1 rejected).}
Calling the vision encoder about five times less often costs 12.0--12.5 points of SR (\cref{tab:sr}); with $k{=}10$ the drop of 9.5 points is not significant after adjustment.
The event trigger removes most gripper chatter (1.2 short segments per 100 steps against 5.0) without recovering success, and fresh proprioception does not substitute for a current image.
A SmolVLA trained from scratch on LIBERO benefits from longer execution horizons~\cite{sendai2025leave}; this checkpoint, fine-tuned with $n_\text{exec}{=}1$, does not.

\paragraph{Re-planning more cheaply preserves success (H2).}
Along released $\to$ float16 $\to$ $S{=}3$ $\to$ graph, a re-plan takes 789, 616, 245 and 112\,ms, factors of 1.28, 2.51 and 2.19 ($7.05\times$ in total; \cref{tab:latency}).
The fits explain these factors.
Without the graph, an Euler step costs 50.5\,ms in bfloat16 and 53.3\,ms in float16, independent of the arithmetic: the loop is launch-bound.
With the graph, it costs 28.3 and 9.4\,ms: removing launches exposes the emulated arithmetic, so the graph gains $1.38\times$ in bfloat16 but $3.46\times$ in float16 at $S{=}10$.
On 200 pairs, neither $S{=}3$ variant differs significantly from the released 77.0\%, but for float16 the lower bound ($-5.8$) does not yet clear the margin.

\paragraph{Trade-offs and next bottleneck.}
The float16 gain is specific to GPUs without bfloat16 tensor cores; elsewhere only the step and graph gains would apply (not measured).
Float16 changes actions by 0.010 on average and 0.049 at most (mean magnitude 0.17), with no overflow observed; the graph needs static shapes and one capture per $S$.
At 112\,ms per re-plan plus 32\,ms of simulation the loop runs at about 7\,Hz, below the 20\,Hz control rate.
By the fit $T(S)\approx84+9.4S$\,ms, the vision-language prefix takes 75\% of a re-plan at $S{=}3$ and even $S{=}1$ would need 93\,ms; further gains require a cheaper prefix, which changes the model and needs training.
